We understand your reality

What's stopping you from filling up.

Before we talk solutions, let's talk about what you really deal with day to day.

Empty services, saturated ones

A Tuesday with twelve covers and a Saturday turning people away is not a customer problem, it is a distribution problem. And it is the quiet service that costs, because the staff are there regardless.

Absent at the moment of choice

People choose a restaurant on their phone, thirty minutes ahead, searching for a cuisine and a place. If your listing has neither recent photos nor an up-to-date menu, the choice is made without you.

Reviews you endure

A negative review left unanswered does more harm than ten positive ones do good. Most places only reply to the ones that annoy them, and too late.

Photos that do not do you justice

The plate looks beautiful in the room and dull on screen. In hospitality, the image makes the booking: an average photo costs covers every day.

Posting when there is time

Which is to say never during the season, and a great deal in winter. Yet your presence needs to be strongest precisely when you have the least time.

Customers you never see again

Good service, a happy customer — and no way of bringing them back. With no contact list and no reason to return, every service starts again from zero.

How we fix it

The right levers, in the right place.

We activate the channels that truly matter for a restaurant or café.

The Google listing treated as a shopfront

Recent photos of the room and the dishes, an up-to-date menu, exact hours including closures, a booking link. It is the first thing your future customer sees, often the only thing.

Visuals and video shot on site

One shooting session in your own room, then spread across several weeks of posts. A single shoot feeds a quarter.

Active review management

Ask systematically, reply to all of them, and above all reply quickly to criticism. A measured reply to a bad review reassures more than a run of five-star ratings.

Content set to your season

We anticipate the quiet periods, the festive menus, the terrace and the closures. Communication is prepared ahead and publishes itself.

Bringing people back rather than conquering

Collecting contacts, the right message at the right time, an offer on a quiet service. Filling a Tuesday costs infinitely less than finding a new customer.

Local advertising, when it is useful

Short campaigns, tightly drawn geographically, triggered on the periods you need to fill. No year-round advertising subscription.

The context

What makes hospitality different.

Hospitality is the sector where the decision is fastest and most visual. Between the urge and the booking there is often less than an hour, and almost all of it plays out on a phone screen: three photos, a rating, a distance. That is why we put the Google listing and the images well ahead of social media in the order of priorities.

It is also a trade with tight margins and strong seasonality. A setup costing the same every month makes no sense when revenue varies twofold. We prefer to concentrate the effort on the periods that need filling and ease off when the room is full.

Let us be honest on one point: a restaurant generally does not need an agency all year round. What it needs is a solid overhaul — listing, photos, reviews, booking — then light support. We are happy to work that way, and we say so rather than selling a subscription that serves no purpose.

Frequently asked questions

What people ask us.

Does social media really bring people in?

It sustains desire and loyalty; it rarely triggers the first visit. That almost always comes from a local search or a recommendation. The common mistake is to bet everything on Instagram while leaving the Google listing to rot: it is the other way round that works.

What should I do about an unfair review?

Reply, calmly, within twenty-four hours, without contesting it point by point. The message is not addressed to its author but to the hundreds of people who will read it. A dignified reply often turns a bad review into proof that you are serious.

I already have an Instagram page — should I start over?

Almost never. We keep the history and the audience, put the presentation straight and set a sustainable rhythm. Starting over loses the followers you have earned, and they are precisely your customers.

How long before it makes a difference?

On the Google listing and the photos, the effect is measured in weeks. On loyalty, you need a full season to judge. We look at covers, not followers.
