We understand your reality

What's holding back your local growth.

Let's start with your day-to-day as a business owner.

People cannot find you

Your customers exist and are searching for your service, but they type a trade and a town. If you appear neither on the map nor in the first results, the call goes to a competitor you will never hear about.

Everything rests on word of mouth

It is an excellent foundation, but it is a channel you do not control. When a large client stops or a season is quiet, there is no tap to turn on.

A website that sleeps

An online brochure never updated, with no visible way to request a quote, generating nothing. It costs hosting every year and brings in no contacts.

Nobody to take care of it

Between jobs, quotes and invoicing, communication comes last. It happens in fits and starts, usually when business slows down — which is to say too late.

Quotes that go unanswered

The problem is not always the price. A quote sent with no follow-up, no references and no clear scope compares badly with one from a better-presented competitor.

No way of knowing what works

A flyer, an ad in the local paper, a sponsorship: without measurement, every outlay is a bet. A year later, nobody knows what to do again.

How we fix it

More customers close to home.

Practical levers for an SME that wants to grow locally.

Local visibility, first

A complete Google listing, real photographs, service area, opening hours, reviews. It is free, it is what your customers look at, and it is almost always the first gain.

A site that asks for the quote

A few well-made pages beat a large empty site: what you do, for whom, in which region, at roughly what price, and a form that genuinely lands in your inbox.

Advertising limited to your area

Campaigns confined to the radius you actually serve, on the searches that precede a purchase. Everything coming from too far away is cut.

Reviews as an asset

Asking for a review is part of finishing the job. We put the habit, the wording and the reply in place: it is what tips the balance against a competitor with no track record.

A steady presence, not a heroic one

Two posts a week kept up for a year beat a spectacular campaign followed by six months of silence. We produce and we schedule.

One figure a month

How many enquiries, from where, at what cost. One page, readable in two minutes, that tells you what to keep and what to stop.

The context

What makes a local business different.

A local business has an advantage larger organisations do not: its market is small and identifiable. You know where the customers are, you know roughly how many, and you can stop paying for a useless audience. The whole approach consists of drawing the area tightly, then being unavoidable inside it rather than vaguely present everywhere.

The second point is time. In a company of two to fifteen people, there is nobody whose job this is. A setup that demands an hour a day will not survive, however willing everyone is. So we build things that run without you, and we save your time for the two or three moments where it cannot be replaced: your expertise, your face, your work.

Finally, budgets are real but limited. That does not stop us working, it changes the order: we start with what costs almost nothing and pays off quickly, we measure, and we only raise the spend on what has already proved something.

Frequently asked questions

What people ask us.

What budget should I plan for?

There is no single answer, and be wary of anyone who gives you one before listening to you. What is true is that the first gains — Google listing, reviews, well-made pages — take work rather than media budget. Advertising comes afterwards, once we know what converts. We quote after the first conversation, not before.

I have no website — should I start there?

Not always. For many service trades, a complete Google listing with recent reviews brings in more calls than a new site nobody finds. The site becomes essential as soon as you need to explain, reassure, or help someone choose between several services.

Does this work outside the big towns?

Often better. In a village or a small region, competition in local search is weak: becoming the first result there is far quicker than in Lausanne. The volume is lower, but so is the cost of winning a customer.

I am already working at full capacity — what is the point?

Choosing your clients rather than taking what comes. When demand exceeds capacity, the issue is no longer volume but the quality of the work and the level of your prices. That is a different job, and we will say so if it is your situation.
